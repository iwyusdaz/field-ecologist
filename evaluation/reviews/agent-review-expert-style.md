# Agent review: simulated persona expert reviews

Agent review, simulated persona. These are AI-simulated perspectives, not real experts. Treat every finding as a hypothesis to confirm with a real ecologist or teacher. Date: 2026-10-27.
Personas: the two named in plan §10 ("ecology instructor", "research methods lecturer"). I also added "biology teacher" because the task prompt asked for it.

---

## 1. Ecology instructor (Agent review, simulated persona)

1. **Major. The true Bunchgrass cover is inconsistent with the plant that is described and drawn.**
   - `trueCover` uses a 0.55 m footprint radius (`src/systems/population-model.js:101`), which gives 53 % cover. That is a 1.1 m-wide tussock.
   - `content/species.json:91` says tussocks are "15–25 cm across". At 0.81 plants/m² with a 0.1 m radius, real cover would be about 2.5 %.
   - Students who estimate cover correctly from the view will see a huge "error" in the reveal. That teaches them that cover estimation is hopeless, which is the wrong lesson.
   - Fix: align the radius with the species card, or change the card.

2. **Major. Two of five species can't be keyed out in the 3D quadrat.** The Daisy-leaf rosette and the orchid's two basal leaves are below the ground surface (see `agent-review-visual.md`, finding 1). The orchid is the "rare species sampling can miss". In the 3D path the learner can't record it even when it is in the frame.

3. **Major. There is no boundary rule for plants on the quadrat frame.** `countInQuadrat` uses a half-open interval (`population-model.js:162`), but the guide never tells learners how to treat edge plants. Real protocols state a rule, such as counting rooted-inside only, or N and W edges in and S and E edges out. Sedge blades about 0.5 m wide overhang the frame in every screenshot. Fix: add a line to the field guide ("count a plant if its base is inside") and render the base point clearly.

4. **Minor. "Triangular stem base (feels edged, not round)" is a touch cue in a visual-only task** (`species.json:66`). It's harmless as natural history, but it can't be used. Mark it "(in the field)".

5. **Minor. Fen Orchid density is 0.0034 /m²** (34 plants per hectare), so 10 random 4 m² quadrats will almost never hit one. That is the intent, but the reveal should say explicitly that a zero estimate is the expected result for rare species at this effort. I found no such copy in `reveal.js`. Fix: add a note when the orchid estimate is 0.

6. **Minor (honesty check passed).** The fictional-species statement is present (`species.json:3`, field guide). Lighting and HDRI are sourced, the Thomas process is cited (`research/sources.md` #4), and "Ashcombe Meadow" is fictional and isn't presented as real. No regional-flora claims.

## 2. Research methods lecturer (Agent review, simulated persona)

1. **Major. The stratified estimate is the unweighted pooled mean, but the allocation is unequal.**
   - `estimates()` divides total count by total area (`src/systems/data-sheet.js:86-98`).
   - Stratified allocation gives "extras to the wet stratum" (`src/systems/designs.js:48-49`): 4 wet, 3 mid, 3 dry for n = 10.
   - With equal-area strata, the correct estimator is the mean of the stratum means. The pooled version over-weights the wet stratum, which biases Blue Sedge upward. That undermines the lesson that stratification is unbiased.
   - Fix: compute Σ(W_h · ȳ_h) for the stratified design, and show it in the report as the stratified estimator.

2. **Major. A blank entry is treated as "not recorded", not as zero.** A species with no entries gets no estimate (`data-sheet.js:91-95`), and the reveal shows "—" instead of 0. Learners who leave zeros blank, the natural habit, lose the estimate. If they type 0 in some quadrats and leave others blank, those quadrats still count in the area denominator, so the omission is invisible. Fix: treat blank as 0 once "Save" is pressed, or require an explicit 0, and say so on the form.

3. **Major. Stated bias vs measured bias.** The README claims "+~420 % bias". That figure comes from the modelled convenience generator: 60 % near clusters, 40 % near the gate (`designs.js:66-72`). Learners using manual convenience placement in 3D aren't generated by that model, yet the simulator always uses the modelled version. Fix: label the simulator row "modelled convenience sampler (not your clicks)".

4. **Minor. The S3 caption says the pile "builds", but it doesn't** (realism review, finding 3). For a statistics lecturer, seeing the distribution emerge is the main point of the simulator.

5. **Minor (passed).** Definitions match OpenStax (`sources.md` #3). The systematic design has a random start, and the simple random design allows one redraw "before any visit" (`fieldwork.js:66-75`), a sensible anti-gaming rule. The transfer items map cleanly to survey sampling.

## 3. Biology teacher, Grades 9–12 (Agent review, simulated persona)

1. **Major. The lesson doesn't fit one 45–60 minute period as claimed.** By my count: 5 pre-check items, 8 ID items, 10 quadrats each with up to 5 inputs, the reveal, a simulator run, 3 chart items, 4 report sections, a rubric and 4 transfer items. That's well over 60 minutes for Grade 9. I estimated this and didn't time it. Fix: give teachers a "short path" (6 quadrats, skip the rubric) in `design/teacher-guide.md`.

2. **Major. The 3D counting step will produce wrong data for reasons that have nothing to do with sampling.** Hidden species and a form covering the frame mean those errors come from the interface, not from the sampling. Class CSVs pooled for discussion would carry those artefacts.

3. **Pass (verified).** The "4 · The reveal" tab is clickable before submission, but the locked state explains itself: "Submit your fieldwork in Module 3 first…" (`src/ui/reveal.js:31-34`).

4. **Minor (passed).** NGSS HS-LS2-2 wording is verified (`sources.md` #1). I found no compliance claims ("aligned with" only), and charts carry data tables.
